# FivePaths voice

The rules for anything with the FivePaths name on it. The visual system is
built on restraint and the prose reads the same way. The full guide is
`WRITING_GUIDE.md` in the `cdn.fivepaths.com` repository; this is the working
subset for a shared document.

## Hard rules

- **No em-dashes.** Use a comma, a colon, or a new sentence.
- **Plain words.** Skip AI-flavoured filler: "seamless", "robust", "empower",
  "elevate", "delve", "honest", and their relatives. If a sentence would
  survive on any company's site, it is too generic for ours.
- **Eyebrow tags are seasoning, not structure.** A couple per document at
  most, on the sections that need orientation. Not every `h2` gets one.
- **No throat-clearing.** "The pattern is consistent", "what follows is the
  machinery behind X", and their kin announce a point instead of making it.
  State the fact. Length is fine; indirection is not.

## Patterns that read as slop

- **The orphan tagline.** A pithy phrase used exactly once is dead weight.
  Coin it and bring it back later, or cut it.
- **The negation triplet.** "No X, no Y, no Z" is a sales-deck bullet crammed
  into a sentence. If a point needs three negations, it needs one concrete
  description instead.
- **The decorative contrast.** "X is not Y; it is Z" earns its place only when
  Y is a misconception the reader would actually hold. If nobody would have
  assumed Y, cut it. A handful across a document is fine; more reads as a tic.
- **Borrowed consulting jargon.** "Leverage", "bench", "scale amplifier",
  "upsell". Naming a buzzword to reject it still leaves its vocabulary doing
  the work. Describe what actually happens.

## Texture

- Headings are plain descriptive labels for what follows, not taglines.
- Use the precise word over the common one only when precision earns it: the
  larger word must tell the reader something the plainer one would not.
- Sentence length follows the information, not a rhythm you impose. A sentence
  is as long as what it carries requires, never padded or clipped.
- Do not repeat information already stated elsewhere in the document.
- Assume the reader is competent and interested. Do not over-explain or hedge.

## In a shared document specifically

The reader signed in to reach this page, so they know who sent it. Open with
the substance, not with a greeting or a recap of the engagement. Say what the
document is and what they should do with it, then get on with it.

State findings as findings. If something is uncertain, say what is uncertain
and why, once, in plain terms.
